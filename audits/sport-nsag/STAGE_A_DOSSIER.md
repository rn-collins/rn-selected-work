## 2. Executive verdict

SPORT identifies a meaningful governance problem: schedules, travel, facilities, culture, public exposure and career transitions are often managed separately even though they jointly affect athlete welfare. The six-domain structure could support a strong institutional inquiry if co-designed with athletes, clinicians, sport scientists, safeguarding experts, labor/legal stakeholders and accessibility specialists.

The current page is not an assessment or operating advisory practice. The diagnostic is an unvalidated five-question self-rating with a completion defect; the promised full report is only a contact modal. There are no operational criteria, sources, weights, norms, reliability, data process, report, roadmap, client, or outcome. The page markets $8,500–$35,000+ engagements and says the pipeline includes F1 and LA28, but no relationship exists in inspected evidence.

Science copy is too deterministic and sometimes physiologically reductive. Governance influences conditions; it does not directly or uniformly control nervous-system state, performance, injury or welfare. Release must be held until evidence, professional review, athlete participation, privacy/safeguarding, service delivery and accessibility are real.

## 7. Copy and information architecture

Lead with institutional conditions and athlete voice, not “performance advantage.” Separate welfare governance, clinical care, sport science, coaching and legal compliance. Mark every example hypothetical. Remove placeholders, unsupported certainty, competitive fear framing and invented pipeline implications. Publish “what we assess / what we do not assess / who reviews / what data we need / how athletes participate / what happens with risks.”

## 8. Science, health and sport evidence

The full evidence ledger is in the route ledger. Required corrections include multilevel performance framing, qualified sleep evidence, no generic injury percentage, no binary autonomic recovery model, careful allostatic-load use, individualized stress/recovery, and non-causal language for culture/agency/media/facility claims. Evidence must be claim-level, dated, source-linked and graded; NCAA guidance is not outcome proof.

## 9. Legal, policy and regulatory boundaries

This is not legal advice, but the current one-line banner is inadequate. Engagement design needs jurisdiction/level-specific review of athlete employment/student status, CBAs, NCAA/conference rules, Title IX, ADA, FERPA, privacy/biometrics, minors/SafeSport, licensing/scope of practice, medical autonomy, discrimination/retaliation, record access, incident reporting and research boundaries. Governance recommendations cannot override clinicians, collective bargaining or athlete rights.

## 10. Privacy, safety and security

Before collecting any athlete information, complete a data protection impact/threat model; minimize and purpose-limit data; separate identity from reporting; define consent in power-imbalanced settings; prevent coach/performance-selection misuse; prohibit covert surveillance; disclose vendors/retention/access/deletion; encrypt/log; establish breach, grievance, safeguarding, clinical and mandatory-reporting escalation. Wearables must not be treated as objective nervous-system truth.

## 11. Accessibility and inclusive design

Add skip/main, focus-visible, semantic/live diagnostic feedback, completion enforcement, non-color result explanation, accessible modal, reduced motion, readable source tables, accommodations and accessible booking/report formats. Test keyboard, screen reader, zoom/reflow, contrast and mobile. Athlete disability/neurodiversity, language, culture, sex/gender, race, age and economic power belong in assessment design without pathologizing or discriminatory profiling.
