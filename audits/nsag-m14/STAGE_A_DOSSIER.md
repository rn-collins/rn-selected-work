## 2. Executive verdict

**C — NSAG component, release-blocked.** M14 has a distinctive and worthwhile purpose: prompt mission planners to consider legal authority, crew autonomy, health governance, accountability and reintegration before long-duration human operations. It should not exist as a maturity assessment in its present form.

The page’s foundational claim—institutions operate in a “legal vacuum”—is false. Space governance has gaps, conflicts and speculative frontiers, but binding treaties, national authorization/licensing, registration/jurisdiction, liability rules, agency requirements, employment/contracts, research/medical ethics, and mission-specific agreements already apply. The 12-item instrument measures only whether generic documents allegedly exist and are periodically reviewed. It cannot score interplanetary jurisdiction or readiness. The promised PDF is 404, the “full dimension report” is six generic labels, and privacy/API/accessibility defects persist.

## 5. Consumer journeys and observed results

### Mission/governance professional

The page creates urgency and offers six understandable categories. A specialist immediately encounters category errors: present law and speculative settlement design are merged; “institution” and mission are undefined; robotic, crewed orbital, lunar, Mars, commercial transport, habitat and resource operations are treated alike. The questions ask only whether documents exist—not whether they identify the correct state, registry, licensing authority, operator, vehicle, personnel, mission phase, or governing instrument.

### Assessment journey

The embedded flow is technically simpler and more functional than modules using blocked Chart.js: one 1–5 answer auto-advances. But users cannot go back, review, choose unknown/N/A, cite evidence, or understand what each scale point means legally. Email is mandatory for the result despite “No email required to start.” The output provides a tier and “Dim 1” through “Dim 6,” not the promised full dimension report or action.

### PDF journey

After a minimally checked email, the page sends a lead, claims “Opening your download…,” and triggers `/pdfs/NSAG_M14_Overview.pdf`; that route is 404. HTTP API success is not checked.

## 7. Copy and information architecture

Replace “interplanetary jurisdiction assessment” with **“M14 Human Spaceflight Governance Issue-Spotting Reflection.”** Put a visible status key on every claim: binding international law; national law/regulation; agency/contractual requirement; non-binding accord/standard; proposed practice; speculative future design.

## 8. Science, law, policy and regulatory claim ledger

### Existing international law omitted or minimized

- **Outer Space Treaty:** Article VI assigns states international responsibility for national activities, including nongovernmental activities, and requires authorization and continuing supervision; VII addresses launching-state liability; VIII preserves jurisdiction/control of the state of registry over registered objects and personnel; V concerns astronauts; IX requires due regard, avoidance of harmful contamination/adverse Earth-environment changes, and consultations. This is not a vacuum.
- **Rescue Agreement, Liability Convention, Registration Convention:** assistance/return, state-to-state liability rules, and registration are central. The Moon Agreement also exists though participation is limited. None appears in the instrument.
- **ISS governance:** the ISS Intergovernmental Agreement, MOUs, implementing arrangements, crew code of conduct and criminal-jurisdiction framework demonstrate existing human-spaceflight governance, even if they do not answer future-settlement questions.

### National authorization and supervision

Article VI operates through national regimes. For US-linked missions, relevant authorities may include FAA commercial launch/reentry licensing, FCC spectrum, NOAA remote sensing, export controls, NASA contractual requirements, and other mission/payload reviews. Applicable law turns on launching/registry states, operator nationality, contracts, mission architecture and activity. “Documented jurisdiction policy” is not a substitute for licenses or continuing supervision.

### Liability, registration and disputes

The Liability Convention differentiates absolute launching-state liability for surface/aircraft damage from fault-based liability for damage elsewhere in space; treaty claims are state-to-state and do not exhaust contracts, domestic tort, indemnity, insurance and cross-waivers. Registration/jurisdiction does not confer sovereignty over celestial territory. Disputes may involve diplomatic claims, claims commissions, domestic courts, contracts/arbitration, employment procedures and mission-command processes. M14 tests none of this.

### Property and resources

OST Article II bars national appropriation. National laws including the US Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act recognize certain rights in extracted resources without claiming sovereignty; other states and the Artemis Accords take positions, while international interpretation and coordination remain contested. Land/property, safety zones/deconfliction, heritage and resource rights must be labelled current, national, nonbinding or unsettled—not converted into a maturity score.

### Human research, health, labor and privacy

Crew can be government personnel, military, employees, contractors, research participants, passengers or settlers. Applicable employment, disability, occupational-safety, workers’ compensation, licensure, privacy and anti-discrimination law varies. A universal “right to refuse mission directives on documented health grounds” is not established by the cited space instruments and may conflict with emergency/command duties; present it as a proposed governance safeguard requiring mission-specific legal and safety design.

Medical governance must distinguish occupational care from research, informed consent from operational necessity, clinical from commander authority, emergency standards, telemedicine/licensure, medication/device regulation, evacuation impossibility, triage, reproductive health, behavioral health, confidentiality versus fitness-for-duty, longitudinal data, and post-mission duties. For US federally conducted/supported research, Common Rule/NASA IRB and agency rules may apply; consent under hierarchical/constrained conditions requires special attention.

### Planetary protection and environment

The module omits OST Article IX, COSPAR policy, NASA planetary-protection requirements, forward/back contamination, sample return, debris, harmful interference, environmental impact, heritage, and the unresolved challenge of crewed Mars contamination. NASA itself reports knowledge gaps that can make treaty-compatible crewed Mars mission design exceptionally difficult. This is a core M14 domain, not a footnote.

### Scientific claims

The Kanas/Manzey and Stuster materials can inform behavioral risk but cannot establish a causal claim that independent governance “produces significantly better” outcomes without exact study design/effect evidence. Analog environments are not legal validation or direct space-settlement outcome evidence. Quotations appear to be site-authored summaries inside quotation marks; verify verbatim or remove quotation styling.

## 11. Business model and conversion

Credible audiences include space-law/policy programs, aerospace medicine/human-factors teams, mission planners and commercial operators. A legitimate service is a facilitated issue-spotting workshop and mission-specific governance map—not a compliance/readiness score. Before sale, define jurisdiction, mission assumptions, professional team, deliverables, reliance limits, update cadence, confidentiality/export-control boundary, conflicts, insurance and maintenance. Do not imply legal, medical, safety, licensing or mission certification.

## 14. Exact remediation plan

1. In `index.html`, replace vacuum/zero claims; classify each statement as law, national rule, accord, policy, contract, proposal or speculation.
2. Rebuild assessment as issue spotting: actor, mission, launch/registry/operating states, object/payload, crew status, mission phase, activity, authority, licenses, contracts and evidence. Add unknown/N/A and no aggregate maturity tier.
3. Add modules for OST/other treaties; authorization/supervision; registration/jurisdiction; liability/insurance/cross-waivers; crew/employment/research/medical; dispute/due process; planetary protection/environment/debris; resources/property; reintegration/data.
4. Add primary-source ledger with articles, states parties/date, national jurisdiction and review date; distinguish binding/nonbinding/unsettled.
5. Remove email from results. Repair and verify the PDF before advertising; check API HTTP status and show failure/retry.
6. Add privacy/terms, retention/deletion, no-sensitive/controlled-data warning, consent separation and backend audit.
7. Add semantic fieldsets, persistent review/back, focus/live status, accessible modal/menu/copy feedback and device/AT/zoom tests.
8. Canonicalize parent/component content to one source to prevent drift; add automated link/PDF/route tests.

Release only after space-law counsel, mission safety/human-factors, aerospace medicine/research ethics, planetary-protection and privacy/security review; production output must clearly be nonlegal issue spotting, not readiness or certification.
