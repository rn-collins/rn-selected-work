Join and part the towers

Both pictures are the same 1900 postcard of the Leaning Tower, copied. Joined, most people see the right tower lean more. Move them apart and watch what happens.

0 px

Joined: the pair reads as one scene, and the right tower seems to lean more.

What the sources say

The illusion (strong, a demonstration you can check): two identical side-by-side photos of a tower shot from below look like two different leans (Kingdom, Yoonessi and Gheorghiu, 2007).

The gap (a demonstration, no observers measured): spreading the photos apart makes the effect grow a little, then it should break down once they no longer read as one scene; for some viewers it persists far apart (Parovel and Costall, 2018).

Why (debated): perspective and scene grouping (Kingdom et al., 2007), or orientation contrast (Maniatis, 2008, read as an abstract only).
