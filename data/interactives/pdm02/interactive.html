Seeing sound: match the shape, match the shade

Do you hear shapes and see pitches? Try two classic tasks, then see what the studies found.

Sound plays only when you press a button. Tones are made in your browser; no recordings are used, and nothing you choose is sent or stored.

1. Bouba or kiki?

Read each made-up word, then pick the outline it belongs to.

Word: bouba

Well establishedIn an online study of 917 people speaking 25 languages, 72% matched bouba to a round shape and kiki to a spiky one, counting only people who got both. It held with other writing systems too: 63% for non-Roman scripts, 75% for Roman ones (Ćwiek et al., 2022).

2. What shade is this tone?

Press Play a tone, listen, then pick the shade that fits. Do it five times; each tone is a different pitch.

Tone 1 of 5

TonePitchYour shade (1 dark, 5 light)

Well establishedMost people pair higher-pitched sounds with small, light, fast-moving things, and lower pitches with big and dark ones (Spence, 2020). Scientists still disagreeWhy: one idea is that we learn it from the world, where large objects vibrate at lower frequencies and higher sounds tend to come from higher places (McEwan et al., 2024).

One small study18 Hermann's tortoises chose a white wall after a higher tone and a black wall after a lower one on about 61% of trials, against 50% chance (Loconsole et al., 2025).

What this is, and what it is not

These are crossmodal correspondences, links nearly everyone shares. Synaesthesia is a different phenomenon, often confused with them (Spence, 2020). Your answers here are just for play; they say nothing about you.
