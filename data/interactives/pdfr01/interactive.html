What the studies found

  • A clear averageOn average, liking peaked for fractal dimension 1.3 to 1.5, in 220 people shown natural, mathematical and painted fractals (Spehar et al., 2003, as reported by Taylor et al., 2011). Mostly from one research network.
  • Two experiments agreePerson by person, about 50% of 310 students peaked in the middle, about 20% liked the smoothest best, about 20% the roughest, and about 10% showed no clear pattern (Spehar et al., 2016).
  • The statistics hold upNatural scenes mostly sit in this middle range, and human vision is most sensitive there (Viengkham & Spehar, 2018). Still earlyThat this tuning is why fractals feel good.
  • Small, early studiesIn 24 people doing stressful tasks, skin signals rose least beside a D 1.4 image (Taylor, 2006, as reported by Taylor et al., 2011). Small studies, mostly from one group, some with commercial design partners (Robles et al., 2021).
  • Scientists still disagreeWhether Jackson Pollock's paintings are fractal (Jones-Smith & Mathur, 2006; Taylor et al., 2011).

This dial is a toy, not a test: one choice says nothing reliable about you. The studies used paired comparisons of many images.
