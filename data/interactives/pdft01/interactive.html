Find the face

Why does a wall socket look surprised?

Tap or press every object that seems to look back at you. Go with your first impression. Then press Show me.

How the game is built
Every object is drawn twice from the same parts. In one version the parts sit as two eyes above a mouth. In the other the same parts are moved out of that arrangement. The answer marks show which version is which. Your picks are not stored or sent anywhere.
One small study
In 7 adults recorded with MEG, objects that looked like faces drew a response in the brain's face area at about 165 thousandths of a second, about when real faces did. Ordinary objects did not (Hadjikhani et al., 2009).
Two kinds of brain scan agree
In 22 people, the brain first treated illusory faces as face-like, then within about 250 thousandths of a second as ordinary objects. The authors describe a face detector that favours sensitivity over selectivity (Wardle et al., 2020).
One small study so far
Five rhesus monkeys looked longer at objects with illusory faces than at matched objects, and looked at the illusory eyes and mouth (Taubert et al., 2017).
Scientists still disagree on the details
One account says perception is the brain's best guess, and strong expectations can make people see what is not there (Corlett et al., 2019). Whether psychedelic states change face pareidolia has not been tested directly.

Your own picks in this game take seconds, because they include deciding and pressing. The brain's first face response comes far sooner than that. This page has no flashing and no timed pressure.
