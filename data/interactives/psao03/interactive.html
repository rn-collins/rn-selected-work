Sort by seed

On one page of his 1583 book, Andrea Cesalpino of Pisa sorted plants by what sits under one flower: how many seeds or seed cases, and whether they are bare seeds or cases. Below are eight of the plants and kinds he names. Sort them one way, then the other, and watch the groups change.

Keyboard: Tab to a button, Enter to sort. Nothing moves on its own.

What the source says

His key (strong, his own words): one seed, as in the almond, or one seed case, as in the rose; two seeds, as in the ferula kind, or two cases, as in cress; three seeds in the spurges, three cases in bulbous plants; four seeds in horehound, four cases in Siler; many seeds in the chicory kind and thistles, many cases in the pine (Cesalpino, 1583, book 1 p. 29).

What he set aside: colour, smell and taste, which he called accidents because they vary with tending, place and climate. Leaves, stems and roots came second, as servants of the fruit.

This toy (a teaching aid, not a finding): the eight entries are taken from his page; the groups are his. We do not judge which of his groups botanists keep today.
