Before and after the bias check

A 2022 meta-analysis pooled 447 results on nudges, changes to how choices are laid out, and found an average effect of d = 0.43 (Mertens et al., 2022, corrected). Studies that find an effect are more likely to be published, so other researchers re-ran the numbers with a correction for missing studies (Maier et al., 2022). By where it was tested

Bars show Cohen's d to scale from 0 to 0.7. Corrected values are the robust Bayesian model-averaged estimates in Maier et al. (2022), Table 1. Their verdicts: evidence against an effect for information and assistance nudges; undecided for structure nudges; no evidence for an overall effect after correction.

What this shows, and what it does not

  • The size of the average nudge effect depends on how missing studies are handled. Other corrections gave -0.01, 0.07 and 0.08 (Szaszi et al., 2022); the original authors' own check gave 0.31 to 0.08 and they agree the true effect is likely smaller (Mertens et al., 2022, reply).
  • An average cannot say whether a particular menu or screen change will work. Effects vary widely between studies. Scientists still disagree.
