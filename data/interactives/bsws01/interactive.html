Cross the bridge: a slime mould learns to ignore quinine

Physarum polycephalum is one cell with no neurons. In a Tokyo lab, a slime mould had to cross an agar bridge laced with bitter quinine to reach food, once a day for four days, while a control crossed plain agar (Yoneoka & Takamatsu, 2023). Pick a day to see how long each one took.

Day 1: quinine bridge 285 minutes, plain agar 110 minutes.

One typical sample per group, from the caption of Figure 1 in Yoneoka & Takamatsu (2023). Bars to scale from 0 to 300 minutes.

What the evidence says

  • STRONGHabituation in the lab: slime moulds crossed a quinine bridge faster day after day, the change was specific to quinine, and caution returned after two days without it (Boisseau et al., 2016; 200 control, 140 quinine and 70 caffeine slime moulds). A second lab confirmed it (Yoneoka & Takamatsu, 2023).
  • EARLYThe tube network may hold part of the change: cutting or turning the trained network made the cell cross as if it had forgotten. One lab; the mechanism is not worked out (Yoneoka & Takamatsu, 2023).
  • STILL DEBATEDWhether this counts as intelligence, and whether a slime mould can learn by association (Reid, 2023).
