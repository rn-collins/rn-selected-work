Step and think

Walking while doing something demanding changes how people walk. You can feel a small version of this sitting down. Tap a steady beat, like footsteps, first on its own and then while counting backwards. The page draws each tap as a footprint and measures how even your beat stays.

  1. Round 1. Tap a steady, comfortable beat 16 times.
  2. Round 2. Tap the same beat 16 times while counting backwards by 3 from the number shown.
  3. Compare. See which round was more even.

What the studies found

EarlyIn 12 young adults, holding two linked rings apart while walking slowed them by 15.1 percent and made steps more variable, while a listening task slowed them by 2.9 percent (Beurskens et al., 2016).

EarlyIn 30 students on a treadmill, harder memory tasks made both walking and answers worse (Xu et al., 2026).

StrongA short bout of exercise gives a small lift to attention tasks, pooled across 50 studies (Oberste et al., 2019).

Our demoThis page is a demonstration, not a measurement. A finger tap is not a footstep, and one try says nothing about you.
