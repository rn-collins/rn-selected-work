What the source says

Six to seven neighbours (strong, for starlings over Rome): in 10 flocks filmed at the Termini roost, each bird interacted on average with 6.5 neighbours (six to seven), however far apart they were, and not with every bird inside a fixed distance (Ballerini et al., 2008).

Why it matters (simulation, the authors' argument): within the same distance the team counted 10 birds in their densest flock and 1 in the sparsest. In their 2D simulation of a predator attack, stragglers were 43 percent of the pieces under a distance rule and 5 percent under a number rule.

This toy (a model, not a finding): the dots are placed by a fixed pattern, not from the study's data; the reach is set so that the first ringed bird starts with a similar number of neighbours (nine) before you spread the flock; the fixed number is 7, the top of the six to seven the study found.
