Seed head: turn the angle

Each new seed is placed a fixed turn from the one before, a little further out. Change the turn and watch the pattern. Near 137.5 degrees the seeds pack evenly and two families of spirals appear; a fraction of a degree away, the seeds fall into straight spokes.

137.5°

Keyboard: the slider moves by 0.1 degree with the arrow keys. Nothing moves on its own.

What the sources say

Sunflowers (strong): in 657 sunflowers grown by the public, 565 of 768 spiral counts were Fibonacci numbers, 67 had another Fibonacci-type pattern and 136 had none (Swinton and Ochu, 2016).

The sequence (strong): Leonardo of Pisa's Liber abbaci (1202) adds each month's rabbit pairs to the month before: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and on to 377.

This toy (a model, not a finding): the golden angle, 137.507 degrees, is computed here as 360 × (1 − 1/φ). Placing seeds at that turn is a geometric model; Swinton and Ochu note that several models compete to explain how plants do it, and the exceptions help tell them apart.
