# Publication curation record

This record documents how the standing portfolio's editorial evidence was selected. It is an internal QA artifact, not a claim that the portfolio displays every publication.

## Inventory boundary

- **Fat Nugs Magazine:** RN Collins's complete three-page author archive was reviewed. It spans reported Hawaiʻi cannabis coverage, *Ask a Neuroscientist*, psychedelic/cannabis science series, cultural criticism, embodied practice, and architecture/materials coverage.
- **Cannabis Law Report:** the site's RN Collins byline search returned 33 relevant records. Two were duplicate publication records for the same federal-drug-policy article, leaving 31 distinct URLs across an initial ten-piece cannabis law series, a twenty-piece psychedelics/legal-issues series, and an earlier long-form rights essay.

The source sites remain canonical. Their archives may add or correct posts after this portfolio review.

## Selection rule

Pieces were not ranked by prestige or topic alone. The standing set had to demonstrate distinct modes of work: field reporting and relationship-building; Hawaiʻi history, land, and jurisdiction; operational technology and compliance infrastructure; public neuroscience and claim correction; environment and care design; accessible multi-system explanation; equity outcome auditing; cross-agency legal issue spotting; institutional oversight design; economic stress-testing; regulatory compliance review; and constitutional analysis.

## Culturalyst-specific selection

The Culturalyst view leads with three pieces after full-text review:

- **Three Days at the Hawaiʻi Cannabis Expo** — shows how RN enters an unfamiliar community, listens, builds trust, and turns encounters into an ecosystem map.
- **Sovereign Ground** — shows verification of community memory alongside land, history, sovereignty, and unresolved jurisdiction.
- **Equity Program Outcomes** — shows the difference between nominal access and durable participation, a direct bridge to discoverability, resourcing, funding, and institutional recognition.

These are recipient-specific leads. The standing portfolio retains a wider selection so another reader can evaluate RN's scientific, legal, technical, and institutional range.
