Your body makes its own cannabinoids. Two receptors, CB1 and CB2, pick up their signals. Here is how lab work since the 1980s found them.

Your body makes its own cannabinoids.

02 / 07

1990: a receptor gene cloned from rat brain turned out to carry the effects of THC. It is now called CB1. (Hourani and Alexander, 2018, review)

1990: a receptor gene cloned from rat brain turned out to carry the effects of THC.

03 / 07

1992: a molecule in pig brain was found to bind the cannabinoid receptor. It was named anandamide, from Sanskrit ananda, bliss. (Hourani and Alexander, 2018)

1992: a molecule in pig brain was found to bind the cannabinoid receptor.

04 / 07

1993: a second receptor, CB2, was found first in the spleen and immune cells, so it was called peripheral. (Hourani and Alexander, 2018)

1993: a second receptor, CB2, was found first in the spleen and immune cells, so it was called peripheral.

05 / 07

1995: a second body-made cannabinoid, 2-AG, was isolated from dog gut. It acts on both CB1 and CB2. (Abidi et al., 2022; Hourani and Alexander, 2018)

1995: a second body-made cannabinoid, 2-AG, was isolated from dog gut.

Since 2016, labs have mapped these receptors atom by atom. A 2023 team noted no CB1 structure yet held a body-made cannabinoid, so they used a stable look-alike.
