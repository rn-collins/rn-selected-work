Your first night in a new bed usually sleeps worse. Sleep labs see it so often that they plan an extra night to absorb it.

02 / 07

Pooling 53 studies, night one meant slower sleep onset, more waking and less total sleep than night two. (Ding et al., 2022, as reported by Wick et al., 2024)

Pooling 53 studies, night one meant slower sleep onset, more waking and less total sleep than night two.

03 / 07

Dense EEG, 27 people aged 14 to 34: night one showed weaker deep-sleep waves over the right front, faster rhythms on the left. One small study. (Mayeli et al., 2022)

Dense EEG, 27 people aged 14 to 34

04 / 07

Night one was still rough with a week between lab nights, and on the first recorded night at home. 45 and 30 people. (Wick et al., 2024)

Night one was still rough with a week between lab nights, and on the first recorded night at home.

05 / 07

Night watch idea: on night one, part of the left hemisphere slept more lightly and woke people faster to odd sounds. Only small tests so far. (Tamaki et al., 2016)

Night watch idea: on night one, part of the left hemisphere slept more lightly and woke people faster to odd sounds.

06 / 07

Three tests of 11 to 13 people. Frontal recordings of 35 and 30 found asymmetry unrelated to the rough night. Studies disagree. (Tamaki et al., 2016; Wick et al., 2024)

Three tests of 11 to 13 people.

07 / 07

Our reading, untested in hotels: expect night one to be the rough one, and do not judge the whole trip by it.
