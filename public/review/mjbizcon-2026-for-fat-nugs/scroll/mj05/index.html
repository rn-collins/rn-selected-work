In 2017, a National Academies panel read over 10,000 study abstracts on cannabis and health. It made nearly 100 conclusions. Many of them say the evidence is not there yet.

02 / 07

Each conclusion got a grade. Conclusive meant strong evidence from randomized controlled trials. The bottom grade, no or insufficient evidence, meant no conclusion could be made.

Each conclusion got a grade.

03 / 07

Top grades as treatment (conclusive or substantial): adult chronic pain. Also chemo nausea and vomiting, and MS spasticity as patients report it. The last two were for oral cannabinoids.

Top grades as treatment (conclusive or substantial): adult chronic pain.

04 / 07

Substantial evidence of statistical links to harm: crashes after recent use. Lower birth weight with smoking while pregnant. Schizophrenia or other psychoses. A link alone does not prove cause.

Substantial evidence of statistical links to harm: crashes after recent use.

05 / 07

In 2017, too little evidence either way: glioma, other cancers, epilepsy, ALS, Huntington's, Parkinson's motor symptoms, asthma as a harm. FDA approved purified CBD for two rare epilepsies in 2018.

In 2017, too little evidence either way: glioma, other cancers, epilepsy, ALS

06 / 07

Why so many gaps? The committee said rules, including Schedule I status, slowed research. In 2017, research cannabis came only through NIDA, all grown at the University of Mississippi.

Why so many gaps? The committee said rules, including Schedule I status, slowed research.

07 / 07

In Dec 2020 the DEA eased growing cannabis for research and has since approved more growers, NIDA says. Overdose? Call 911 (US) or your local emergency number.
