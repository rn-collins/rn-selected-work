# RN Selected Work: how every word should sound (Nov 27, 2026)

RN's brief: every word earned, every line leading to the next, written for a cold reader. Prose should read like a person
in their twenties wrote it, plainly, with no AI tells and no generational slang. Internet phrases appear only where they
literally fit.

## Who reads this site
1. People who might hire RN: museums, science institutions, brands and AI companies. They ask, "Can she make this for us?"
2. Editors and collaborators. They ask, "What has she published, and is it good?"
3. Curious strangers. They ask, "What is this, and why should I keep scrolling?"

## Prose rules
- Write short, plain, specific sentences. Use "I" for RN. Contractions are fine.
- Each sentence says one new thing. Cut any sentence that repeats one before it.
- Use real numbers, names and dates instead of adjectives ("33 articles in Fat Nugs Magazine", not "extensive writing").
- Lead with what the thing is and who it's for. Say what it does for the reader before saying how it was made.
- End each section with one clear next step.
- Use US spelling: color, organization, license.
- Use RN's name as "RN Collins". Never use her legal name.

## AI tells to remove
- Em dashes in prose. Use a comma, a period or a colon.
- These patterns: "not just X, but Y", "whether you're X or Y", "in today's world", "it's important to note", stacked
  rhetorical questions, "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", and lists of three adjectives.
- These words, unless they're the literal term: delve, seamless, leverage, robust, unlock, elevate, empower, journey,
  landscape, navigate, tapestry, realm, foster, holistic, synergy, cutting-edge, game-changer, harness, streamline,
  transformative, nuanced, multifaceted, testament, underscore, vibrant, meticulous, comprehensive, intricate,
  ever-evolving, "at the intersection of", showcase, curated, ecosystem, paradigm, innovative, impactful, actionable,
  stakeholder.
- Abstract nouns used to sound big: "systems", "frameworks", "institutional", "infrastructure", "space". Name the actual
  thing instead (a website, a guide, a checklist, a class).

## Insider words to remove
Don't use the site's own filing terms on public pages: shelf or shelves, build index, Studio, Hold, Shipped, record (when
it means a page), production URL, canonical, estate, portfolio (say "site" or "work"), surface (as a verb), "Zero to One".

## Internet phrases: only where they literally fit
Use these as room names, labels or buttons, never inside a paragraph:

| Phrase | Use it for | Don't use it for |
|---|---|---|
| TIL ("today I learned") | one surprising, sourced fact | a summary or an opinion |
| TL;DR | a short summary of something longer | a heading over long text |
| AMA ("ask me anything") | inviting questions | a sales pitch |
| Rabbit hole | deep, self-directed research projects | anything short |
| Receipts | sources and evidence | anything that isn't checkable |
| Sound on | something that actually plays sound | silent video |
| Hear me out | a pitch for an idea | instructions |
| Try it yourself | an interactive you can use | a link to read more |

Never use generational slang: no cap, slay, bussin, rizz, it's giving, lowkey, highkey, bestie, periodt, ate, understood the
assignment, main character.

## Never rewrite
Titles of other people's work, quotes, citations, legal names of documents, museum records, image credits, and the
names of RN's own projects.

## How the audit runs
Every visible string on every page is pulled from the live site (the crawl in the scratchpad: 1,011 pages and 21,866
distinct strings). Each string gets a verdict in the audit ledger: keep, rewrite (with the new text) or cut, plus the
rule behind it. Rewrites are applied where the string lives (lib/*.ts, data/*.json or a page), and the crawl runs again
to confirm.
